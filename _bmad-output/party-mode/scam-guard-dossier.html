Project Dossier · Scam-Guard

Decisions From the Founding Session

What scam-guard is, the tiers it ships as, how it detects and responds, and what's still unresolved — compiled from a live working session grounded in a real incident.

v1.4 2026-09-29 Compiled by the founding round-table
01

Mission — two goals, held together

Most people have no idea how to protect themselves from a scam, and no idea what to do once one is already underway. That's the gap. Two goals were set to close it, deliberately kept as one plan rather than split into "the good version" and "the business version."

Goal 1 — Civic

Baseline protection, free, for everyone

Zero friction, zero required action from the person. An agent that watches, warns, and blocks on its own — no cloud dependency for the core promise, no basic-feature paywall.

Goal 2 — Business

Enterprise-grade protection, sold to individuals

Paid tiers bring the correlation, coordination, and response methods that already work for enterprises down to a single family — funding the free tier and the company both.

02

The tier ladder

Each tier is a real capability threshold, not just a price point — what the agent is allowed to see and coordinate with changes at every step.

FREE

Solo Sentinel

One agent per device. Receives updates, connects to the cloud, prevents and protects locally, reports what it finds upward.

Reports up, doesn't coordinate across devices.
MID

Coordinated Pack

A person's agents start acting as a team — syncing what each device sees and exchanging signals, so a laptop can warn based on what the phone just saw.

Adds cross-device coordination and action.
TOP

Family Watch

Multiple people, one plan. Role-based access across a bounded seat count — who can see what about whom is a defined, deliberate setting, not a default.

Adds other people and access control.
GOLD

Concierge Response

A human investigator for a specific, high-severity case — the call you actually need to make when something has already gone wrong.

Adds a person, not just software.
03

Response model — severity, then proof

Action scales with severity. A suspicious SMS gets a warning. An attempt to take control of a device gets killed immediately, unless the other side can prove it's legitimate.

Decided

Human-to-human verification

"Is this really my family, on this call, right now?"

A native, rotating shared signal — one screen, one number, paired once in person. Rejected a static shared family password: a secret that never changes is just a script a scammer can talk someone into repeating, the same failure mode as OTP-relay fraud.

Decided

Device / session legitimacy

"Is this remote-control attempt really authorized by the device owner?"

Resolved onto an existing standard rather than a bespoke one: FIDO2 / WebAuthn passkeys — the same passwordless cryptographic proof already used to unlock banking apps and phones. Open standard, open-source server implementations available, no new secret for anyone to learn or phish.

Design principle: every block gets one calm, unmissable moment — "we stopped this — was this you?" — with a single-tap way back. This is the one screen the whole product's trust depends on.

Severity scoring

Every signal gets two numbers before it gets a response: Severity (1–5, how bad if real) and Confidence (1–5, how sure this signal alone means real). Risk = Severity × Confidence (1–25). Risk ≥ 18 acts alone — straight to KILL, no correlation needed.
Adopt, don't reinvent
This is a smaller, rougher version of CVSS (Common Vulnerability Scoring System — Exploitability × Impact, the industry-standard severity model maintained by FIRST). Worth adopting CVSS's actual metric structure, or FIRST's decision-tree alternative SSVC, before this scoring model ships as-is.

Escalation rule

Everything under 18 needs company. Within a rolling 90-minute window, take the highest Risk per layer touched (Install / Message / Account / Call-pattern), then multiply by how many distinct layers fired — a signal appearing in several layers at once is disproportionately more likely to be one coordinated attack than several coincidences.
Layers touched in windowMultiplier
1 layer×1.0
2 layers×1.6
3+ layers×2.5
Composite ≥ 40 → promote every open WARN in the window to KILL

Unconditional overrides

A signal only qualifies here if no legitimate institution, family emergency, or support process would ever produce it — the false-positive cost of blocking it is effectively zero. These bypass scoring entirely.

U1

Relay a one-time passcode to anyone

No legitimate party ever needs a code read back to them.

Unconditional
U2

Pay by gift card, crypto, or wire transfer to "unlock" or "verify" an account, or to prevent an arrest

Named directly in FTC consumer guidance — no official body accepts these as payment.

Unconditional
U3

Told to move your own money into a new "safe account" the caller provides

Banks freeze accounts; they never ask you to relocate funds "for protection."

Unconditional
U4

Told to keep it secret from family, or discouraged from hanging up to verify

No real emergency requires isolating the recipient from people who could help them think clearly.

Unconditional
U5

"Law enforcement" demanding immediate payment or threatening instant arrest by phone or text

Real agencies mail notices and use courts, not ultimatums.

Unconditional
U6

Asked to install screen-sharing to "receive" a refund, prize, or unexpected payment

The inverted tech-support trick — nobody needs your screen to give you money.

Unconditional
U7

A financial transaction executes while a remote-control session is active

Not a request anymore — an observed event. Freeze first, ask later.

Unconditional
U8

An attempt to disable or uninstall the agent itself right after a WARN or KILL

Standard endpoint-protection doctrine — a real attacker tries to kill the guard dog first.

04

Detection architecture — three layers, one correlation

Live call-audio analysis was deliberately dropped from scope (see Positioning, below). What's left is three signal layers, none of which is the product on its own — the correlation across them is.

INSTALL

Install-layer signals

A remote-access tool appears and is granted control — especially right after a call or message urging it.

MESSAGE

Message-layer signals

SMS or chat from an unverified sender carrying urgency, a payment ask, or a link — especially arriving in a burst right after a call.

ACCOUNT

Account-layer signals

A password reset, a recovery-info change, a new-device login — especially soon after install-layer activity.

The actual product: any one of these alone is a weak signal. All three, clustered in under an hour, is a composite incident — the same correlation model enterprise SIEM/XDR tools already run, applied to a household. A confirmed pattern becomes a "recipe" distributed to every other agent, the same way antivirus vendors distribute malware signatures — applied here to social-engineering scripts instead of code.
Adopt, don't reinvent
MISP (Malware Information Sharing Platform) is a free, open-source platform built for exactly this kind of distribution, on open standards — STIX to structure an indicator, TAXII to transport it. Publish confirmed patterns in STIX format over MISP-compatible infrastructure rather than building a bespoke propagation protocol.

Fleet-wide confirmation policy

MISP/STIX is a transport, not a policy — it doesn't say when a pattern earns the broadcast. A candidate pattern starts as a Rumor (local, unconfirmed) and is promoted to Confirmed (fleet-broadcast) only once independent corroboration clears a threshold. The threshold isn't one fixed number — it's the same Severity axis reused: confirmation requirement scales with the blast radius of being wrong.
If the pattern is wrong...Confirmation requirement
Low harm (e.g. a phishing link/shortcode)Few independent reports, auto-promotes fast
High harm (e.g. flags a legitimate remote-access tool)Many independent, trust-weighted reports plus a human review gate before broadcast
Sybil defense
"Independent" means distinct accounts and devices with no shared trust graph — a family plan's five devices are one opinion, not five. Reports are weighted by device reputation (age and clean history on the same FIDO2/WebAuthn-anchored identity already adopted for session legitimacy) and rate-limited per device, so a patient sock-puppet farm can't buy influence by queuing one-off votes over time.
05

Positioning — what to cede, what to own

Live, in-call audio analysis was the original vision for the message layer. It was deliberately dropped after research surfaced two facts that made it the wrong hill.

GOOGLE OWNSOn-device, real-time scam-call detection is already shipping (Pixel 9+, Gemini Nano), plus OS-level spoofed-caller verification over RCS.
PLATFORM BLOCKSBoth iOS and Android now block third-party apps from live call-audio access at the policy level, not just the technical one.
DECISIONCede the live-call layer to OS vendors. Differentiate on cross-signal correlation, coverage on the phones and platforms they've abandoned, the family layer, and a human on the other end of the gold tier — none of which is Google's business to build.
06

The reference incident

Every pattern below is drawn from one real, coordinated attack on a family, told here in general form. It's the reason this document exists.

A spoofed call impersonating a known contact opened a night of escalating panic — a fabricated medical emergency, threats, and deliberately dropped calls designed to prevent verification. In parallel, a second call impersonating the family's telecom provider talked a parent through installing a remote-access tool, using real personal details to sound credible. Bank one-time codes arrived by SMS; no funds moved. Once the device was under outside control, the attacker pivoted to resetting email and social account passwords.

07

Open questions

Named and deliberately left open, rather than papered over.

Family RBAC and dignity

Who holds admin by default in the Top tier, and what does a monitored family member get to see about their own monitoring? Parent-over-minor is an accepted category; adult-child-over-aging-parent is a far more delicate one.

Offensive measures — how far, not who

The personnel question is closed (see Business Model, below): honeypot/counter-scam work is staffed by legitimate offensive-security professionals, never by people with an active or recent fraud record. What's still open is how far the legitimate function goes — honeypots and infrastructure/registrar/ISP reporting are uncontroversial; direct counter-strikes on scam-center infrastructure are not.

Unresolved tension in the room: framed as a differentiator by one voice, redirected toward lawful levers by another.

Revisit live-call detection

Ceded to OS vendors for now — not ruled out permanently. A carrier partnership or an app-native VoIP calling feature could reopen this later on legitimate footing.

08

Business model — who actually pays

The free tier isn't charity funded by the paid tiers alone — the strongest funding source is whoever already eats the financial loss when a scam succeeds.

Revenue: bundled benefit, not a hard sell

Primary path is B2B2C through banks, insurers, and telecoms — each already carries a balance-sheet reason to fund fraud prevention (Reg E reimbursement liability, identity-theft/elder-fraud insurance riders, spoofed-call regulatory pressure). Scam-Guard becomes a white-label benefit bundled into an existing account ("free with your bank account"), not a $9.99/mo ask a scared family has to discover and trust on its own. Direct-to-consumer Mid/Top/Gold tiers (see §02) remain the individual-purchase path where no institutional bundler exists yet.

Bridge funding: non-dilutive, before enterprise contracts land

FTC consumer-protection initiatives, DOJ Elder Justice Initiative funding, and AARP Foundation elder-fraud-prevention grants are real, existing, non-dilutive sources sized for exactly this problem shape — usable to build and prove the free tier before a bank signature is realistic.

Government and police: intel membership, not a data hand-off

Direct government/police funding moves on procurement timelines, not product timelines — treat it as slow, not primary. More importantly: they will not hand over collected fraud data — bank SAR filings are legally privileged, and police case data is protected evidence or regulated PII, not a data source either side can just share. The real door is the one already opened by adopting MISP/STIX (§04): become a contributing/consuming member of an existing threat-intel consortium — FS-ISAC for banking, aggregated (not case-level) reporting to the FBI's IC3 — feeding the confirmed-pattern signal in, not requesting raw case files out.
Guardrail
Whoever signs the check should not silently become who the product answers to. The block-screen dignity principle (§03 — "we stopped this, was this you?", one-tap way back) holds regardless of which institution is the paying customer; a bundler's fraud-loss dashboard is not grounds to make it naggier or less person-first.

Offensive/investigative staffing

Honeypot and counter-scam work (the Gold-tier concierge and threat-intel layers) is staffed by legitimate offensive-security professionals — same hiring discipline as any red team: insurable, background-checkable, normal employment law. The O2 "Daisy" campaign is the model to copy: voice actors and scripted bots aimed at scam infrastructure, not real former scammers on payroll. Knowingly employing or contracting people with an active or recent fraud record as a "reformed hacker" pipeline is rejected — due-diligence liability, insurer exclusions, and conspiracy-exposure risk outweigh the redemption-story upside.

Pitch framing

To investors: infrastructure story, not charity story — "the sensor at the edge feeding the fraud-intelligence systems banks, insurers, and telecoms already run," using the same adopt-don't-reinvent standards (MISP/STIX, FIDO2, CVSS/SSVC) already chosen elsewhere in this dossier.
